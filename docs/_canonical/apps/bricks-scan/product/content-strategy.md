# Bricks Scan — Content Strategy

> This document is the single source of truth for all social media content.
> It is used by both the human team and the Content Pipeline Agent.
> Update it when product positioning, audience understanding, or platform strategy changes.

---

## The App

**Bricks Scan** is a native Apple document scanning and organization app for iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

It scans documents, extracts text on-device with OCR, organizes them with folders and tags, generates AI summaries using Apple Intelligence, and exports to PDF, JPEG, text, or HTML.

Everything runs on Apple frameworks. No third-party dependencies. No data leaves the device.

**Current version:** v1.0
**Platforms:** iOS, iPadOS, macOS
**Available on:** App Store

For the full product overview, see [overview.md](./overview.md). For positioning, see [positioning.md](./positioning.md).

## Who We're Talking To

### Primary Audience

**Privacy-conscious Apple users** — people who choose apps based on what they *don't* do with your data. They've read about cloud-based OCR services sending documents to external servers. They know the difference between on-device and server-side processing. They want control.

**Apple power users** — people who care about how software is built. They appreciate native SwiftUI, Apple Intelligence integration, and iCloud sync done right. They notice when an app feels truly native versus when it's a wrapped web view. They follow indie developers, read release notes, and understand what "no third-party dependencies" actually means.

### Who We Are Not Talking To

- Casual users who don't think about privacy or platform quality
- Enterprise buyers evaluating compliance or fleet deployment
- People looking for the cheapest option

---

## What Makes Bricks Scan Different

These are the genuine differentiators. Content should reinforce them — not as marketing claims, but as real facts.

1. **On-device everything.** OCR, AI summaries, metadata extraction, and image processing all happen on the device. No document ever touches an external server.
2. **Apple-native, no compromises.** Built entirely on Apple frameworks — Vision, VisionKit, Foundation Models, SwiftData, StoreKit 2, AppIntents. Zero third-party dependencies.
3. **Apple Intelligence integration.** Document titles, summaries, and tag suggestions are generated by on-device Foundation Models — not a cloud API.
4. **Cross-platform with a native feel.** One app on iPhone, iPad, and Mac. Each platform feels designed for it, not adapted to it.

## Brand Voice

### What Bricks Scan Sounds Like

- Calm and direct. No excitement for its own sake.
- Technically confident without being condescending.
- Practical. We say what the thing does, why it matters, and stop.
- Trustworthy. We don't oversell. We don't manufacture urgency.

### What Bricks Scan Does Not Sound Like

- Hype: "game-changing", "revolutionary", "the future of", "supercharge", "unlike anything else"
- Pressure: "Don't miss this!", "Limited time", "You need to see this"
- Hollow enthusiasm: "Excited to share!", "Big news!", "We're thrilled to announce"
- Vague value: "Better document management", "Seamless experience", "Next-level scanning"

### The Voice Formula

> State what it does. Then why it matters. Then stop.

**Wrong:** "We're thrilled to announce an exciting new feature that will revolutionize how you work with documents!"

**Right:** "Bricks Scan now imports existing PDFs directly. Bring in the documents you already have without re-scanning them."

## Content Pillars

Every post belongs to one of these five pillars.

### 1. Privacy & Control
Posts that highlight on-device processing, no data collection, and user ownership of their documents.

*Angle examples:*
- What happens (and doesn't happen) to your documents when you scan with Bricks Scan
- Why on-device OCR matters for sensitive documents
- The difference between on-device AI and cloud AI for document analysis

### 2. Apple Craftsmanship
Posts that celebrate the quality of native Apple development — for the audience that understands and appreciates it.

*Angle examples:*
- Using Apple Intelligence Foundation Models for document summarization
- How the app uses Continuity Camera on Mac
- Why we built on SwiftData instead of a third-party database

### 3. Practical Usefulness
Posts that show real-world use cases. Helps potential users see themselves using the app.

*Angle examples:*
- Scanning and organizing receipts for taxes
- Keeping contracts and signed documents accessible and searchable
- Using folders and tags to organize medical records or study notes

### 4. Product Updates
Posts that announce what just shipped. Factual, brief, no ceremony.

*Angle examples:*
- New feature releases
- Platform support updates
- Localization additions

### 5. Transparency & Process
Posts that share honest progress, decisions, or thinking behind the product.

*Angle examples:*
- Why we chose a particular technical approach
- What we're working on and why
- Honest reflections on what we got wrong and fixed

## Platform Rules

### X

- **Length:** 280 characters max. Threads only when the idea genuinely needs room.
- **Tone:** Punchy. One clear idea per post. No padding.
- **Hashtags:** 0–2 max. Only when they genuinely add discoverability (e.g., `#iOS`, `#AppleIntelligence`).
- **What works:** Technical observations, product updates, short value statements.

### LinkedIn

- **Length:** 100–250 words. 3–4 short paragraphs.
- **Tone:** Professional, value-first. More space for context than X.
- **Hashtags:** 2–3 relevant professional hashtags at the end.
- **Opening line rule:** Do not start with "Excited to share" or any hollow opener. Start with the most interesting sentence.
- **What works:** Use case stories, privacy/security angles, product philosophy, developer craft.

### Instagram *(planned — not active in V1)*

Not yet activated. Requires a visual content library. Do not include in scheduled posts until activated.

## Weekly Cadence

**Target:** 3–5 posts per week across all active platforms.

| Day | Platform | Pillar |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | LinkedIn | Practical Usefulness or Product Update |
| Wednesday | X | Privacy & Control or Apple Craftsmanship |
| Friday | X | Transparency & Process or Product Update |

Adjust when something ships. A major release replaces the scheduled post with a Product Update across both platforms.

---

## Off-Limits

- Negative comparisons to named competitors
- Claims about user numbers or revenue unless verified and approved
- Political commentary unrelated to privacy or digital rights
- Engagement bait ("Like if you agree", "Tag someone who needs this")
- Exaggerated or unverifiable claims
- Announcements of features that haven't shipped yet
